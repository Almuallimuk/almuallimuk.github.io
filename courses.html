Questions about our courses

Which Quran course should my child start with?

If your child cannot yet read Arabic letters, start with Noorani Qaida. Once they can read joined words fluently, they move to Qur'an Reading with Tajweed. Hifz begins only after Tajweed reading is solid.

Can I switch courses later?

Yes. Courses are designed as a sequence, so students naturally progress from Noorani Qaida into Tajweed, and from Tajweed into Hifz.

Do all courses use the same teachers?

Yes, every course is taught by the same pool of qualified, vetted teachers — including female tutors for Sisters-Only Classes.

Are the courses suitable for adults?

Yes. Adult Quran Classes are designed for adults returning to reading or starting from scratch, and all other courses are open to adults too.

How long is each course?

Noorani Qaida typically takes 4–8 months, Tajweed reading 6–12 months, and Hifz is a multi-year commitment.

مَاذَا سَتَتَعَلَّم؟

What you'll learn in Noorani Qaida

Every lesson builds a small, solid step — no rushing, no skipping.

The 28 Arabic letters

Recognising every letter in its isolated form, and hearing the difference between similar-sounding letters.

Makhraj (correct pronunciation)

Where each letter is pronounced from — throat, tongue, lips — so recitation is accurate from day one.

Harakat (vowels)

Fatha, kasra, damma, sukoon and shadda — the short marks that change how a letter sounds.

Joining letters

How letters connect at the beginning, middle and end of a word — the bridge to reading real Arabic.

Madd (elongation)

Stretching vowel sounds for the correct number of counts — the first real Tajweed rule.

Reading practice words

Short Qur'anic words and phrases so students feel the progress before moving to full verses.

الْمَنْهَج

The Noorani Qaida curriculum

Taught in three stages, each with clear milestones.

  1. Stage 1 — Letters & sounds

    Learning each Arabic letter by shape and sound. Students practise hearing and repeating, and start writing letters to reinforce recognition.

  2. Stage 2 — Harakat & joining

    Short vowels, sukoon, shadda, and how letters join into two-, three- and four-letter words. This is where reading clicks for most students.

  3. Stage 3 — Madd & first verses

    Elongation rules and reading short Qur'anic words. By the end of this stage, students are ready to move into Qur'an Reading with Tajweed.

Key Tajweed rules you'll learn

These are the foundational rules every student covers, with plenty of guided practice in class.

RuleArabicWhat it meansExample
GhunnahغُنَّةNasal sound held for 2 countsإِنَّ (inna)
IdghamإِدْغَامMerging one letter into anotherمِن رَّبِّهِمْ (mir-rabbihim)
IkhfaإِخْفَاءHiding the noon sound before certain lettersأَنْتُمْ (antum)

Listening before reciting
Every Almuallim lesson includes guided listening — the teacher recites, the student repeats, and corrections happen in real time.

الْمَنْهَج

The Tajweed curriculum

Taught in four progressive stages, each with clear milestones.

  1. Stage 1 — Makhraj revision

    A quick, focused review of where each Arabic letter is pronounced from — the foundation Tajweed builds on.

  2. Stage 2 — Noon & Meem rules

    Ghunnah, Idgham, Ikhfa and Iqlab — the rules that govern how ن and م behave around other letters.

  3. Stage 3 — Madd & Qalqalah

    Elongation rules, natural and secondary Madd, and the bouncing Qalqalah letters.

  4. Stage 4 — Fluent recitation

    Applying all rules at speed, with correct pausing (waqf) and starting (ibtida'), reading longer passages independently.

Tajweed — common questions

Do I need to finish Noorani Qaida before starting Tajweed?

Ideally yes. Tajweed assumes you can already read joined Arabic words. If you can read but pronunciation is weak, we can start Tajweed straight away with a short Qaida revision.

How long does it take to learn Tajweed?

With weekly 1-to-1 lessons and 15–30 minutes of daily practice, most students reach confident Qur'an reading with Tajweed within 6–12 months.

What are the main Tajweed rules?

The foundational rules are Ghunnah, Idgham, Ikhfa, Iqlab, Qalqalah and Madd — taught in a clear sequence with plenty of practice.

Is Tajweed compulsory?

Reciting the Qur'an with correct pronunciation is considered obligatory, and Tajweed is the set of rules that makes that possible.

مَاذَا يَتَضَمَّن؟

What the Hifz programme includes

Memorisation alone is not enough — without revision, it fades. Every element works together.

Sabaq (new memorisation)

A small, carefully chosen portion each day, memorised with correct Tajweed from the start.

Sabqi (recent revision)

Revision of the last few days' memorisation, so nothing slips before it settles.

Manzil (long-term revision)

Cyclical revision of older Juz, so the whole Qur'an stays fresh, not just the newest pages.

Weekly targets

Clear, achievable weekly goals agreed with the student and shared with parents.

Monthly progress reports

Parents see exactly which Juz have been completed, which are being revised, and what's next.

Milestone celebration

Completing a Juz, a quarter, or the full Qur'an is marked — motivation matters over years.

الطَّرِيقَة

How the Hifz programme works

A steady, sustainable routine — not a sprint.

  1. Readiness check

    Before starting Hifz, the student must be able to read the Qur'an fluently with Tajweed. If not, we recommend completing Tajweed first.

  2. Daily sabaq

    A small new portion each day — typically a few lines to begin with, growing as the student's memorisation muscle develops.

  3. Daily muraja'ah

    Every lesson includes revision of recent and older portions, with the teacher listening and correcting.

  4. Juz milestones

    Each completed Juz is reviewed thoroughly before moving on, ensuring a solid, lasting foundation.

Hifz — common questions

At what age can a child start Hifz?

Most students begin between ages 7 and 12, after completing Noorani Qaida and Tajweed reading. Readiness matters more than age.

How long does it take to memorise the whole Qur'an?

Full Hifz typically takes 3–6 years with consistent daily practice. The pace is built around the individual student.

What is muraja'ah?

Muraja'ah is revision of previously memorised portions. Without it, memorised verses fade. Every Almuallim Hifz lesson includes dedicated revision time.

Do you offer Hifz for adults?

Yes. Adults can begin Hifz at any age, with a pace and structure designed around work and family commitments.

Islamic Studies — common questions

What is the difference between Tafseer and Islamic Studies?

Tafseer focuses on understanding the Qur'an verse by verse. Islamic Studies is broader: seerah, duas, manners and basics of faith. Almuallim combines both in one course.

Can my child take Tafseer without finishing Tajweed?

Yes. Tafseer focuses on meaning rather than recitation, so it can be studied at any reading level — many students take it alongside Tajweed.

What age is Islamic Studies suitable for?

All ages, with age-appropriate materials. Younger children focus on duas, manners and stories; older students cover seerah and Tafseer in depth.

Do you teach Tafseer in English or Arabic?

Both. Tafseer is taught in English for UK students, with Arabic verses read and explained. Arabic-medium instruction is available on request.

Adult Quran Classes — common questions

Is it too late to learn Quran as an adult?

No. Many Almuallim students are adults starting from scratch or returning after decades. The lessons are designed around adult learning — no judgement, no rush.

Can adults learn Tajweed from scratch?

Yes. Adults who cannot yet read Arabic begin with Noorani Qaida, then progress to Tajweed reading. Adults who can already read can start Tajweed directly.

What if I am embarrassed about my level?

Every Almuallim lesson is one-to-one, so no one else hears you. Teachers are patient and experienced with adult beginners.

Do you offer flexible scheduling for working adults?

Yes. Lessons are scheduled in UK time, with early morning, evening and weekend slots available to fit around work and family.
